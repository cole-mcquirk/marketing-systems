# Audience & Demand Intelligence

Demand generation starts before the campaign, keyword, ad, or piece of content.

The first job is figuring out **who you're actually trying to reach, what makes them relevant, what they need, and what evidence you have for believing any of that.**

I don't think of an ICP as a persona document you make once and forget about. It works better as a living model that gets smarter as customer conversations, sales knowledge, search behavior, CRM data, and actual results accumulate.

## The basic model

I use five main sources of intelligence:

1. Customer reality
2. Internal knowledge
3. Market and search signals
4. Business priorities
5. Behavioral data

These inputs feed the audience model, which then informs content, channels, qualification, conversion, nurture, and measurement.

---

## 1. Customer reality

Start with the people already interacting with the organization.

Useful inputs include:

- Customer interviews
- Existing customer patterns
- Lead inquiries
- Common questions
- Reasons people buy
- Reasons people don't
- How customers describe their own problems

The language customers use is often more useful than the language an organization uses internally.

Knowing who the customer is matters, but so does knowing what pushed them to start searching in the first place.

---

## 2. Internal knowledge

Marketing rarely knows the customer best on its own.

Talk to the people who spend their time with customers:

- Sales
- Customer success
- Technical teams
- Product teams
- Trainers and educators
- Subject-matter experts
- Other customer-facing teams

A useful question is:

> What does someone elsewhere in the organization know about our audience that isn't currently reflected in our marketing data?

Then find a way to structure it.

### Turn knowledge into data

One of the most useful things I've learned is that qualitative knowledge becomes much more powerful when it can be translated into structured data.

Instead of knowing only:

> "This looks like a good lead."

identify **why**.

Depending on the organization, qualification attributes might include:

- Organization type
- Size or volume
- Geography
- Technology used
- Existing capabilities
- Operational model
- Products or services offered
- Current provider or solution
- Buying authority
- Stated needs
- Behavioral engagement

Those attributes can then become:

- CRM fields
- Form questions
- Enrichment data
- Audience criteria
- Lists
- Routing logic
- Lead scoring inputs
- Automation triggers
- Reporting dimensions

The exact fields will be different for every organization.

The important part is creating a shared language between **what people know about the customer** and **what the systems know about the customer**.

---

## 3. Market & search signals

What people search for is another form of customer research.

Search data can reveal:

- Problems people are actively trying to solve
- Questions that appear repeatedly
- Terminology customers actually use
- High-interest topics
- Emerging categories
- Competitor visibility
- Gaps where useful information doesn't exist yet
- Differences between what an organization talks about and what the market actually looks for

SEO research therefore isn't only about ranking pages.

It can help answer:

> What does the market appear to care about, and how does that compare with what we assume it cares about?

Useful inputs include:

- Keyword research
- Search volume
- Search intent
- Competitor rankings
- SERP patterns
- Organic landing-page performance
- Internal site search
- AI and LLM visibility
- Questions appearing repeatedly in search and customer conversations

Search behavior should not dictate strategy on its own, but it is one of the best external signals available for understanding real demand.

### Search as a feedback loop

Search can also help uncover audiences that weren't obvious at the beginning.

A simplified loop looks like:

Existing customer knowledge  
↓  
Search research  
↓  
Useful content  
↓  
Organic visibility  
↓  
New visitors  
↓  
New contacts  
↓  
New audience data  
↓  
Better understanding of the market  
↺

Organic growth therefore isn't only an acquisition channel.

It can create new market intelligence.

---

## 4. Business priorities

Not every high-volume topic deserves attention.

Demand generation has to support the organization.

That means balancing audience demand with things like:

- Strategic products or services
- Priority markets
- Revenue potential
- Organizational capabilities
- Available expertise
- Available resources
- Sales priorities
- Existing strengths
- Areas where the organization wants to build authority

A useful opportunity usually sits somewhere between:

**what the audience wants + what the organization is good at + what the organization wants to grow**

This helps avoid a common SEO and content trap:

> Creating something simply because a keyword has volume.

A lower-volume topic closely connected to a strategic audience or organizational priority may be far more valuable than a broad high-volume topic.

---

## 5. Behavioral data

Once people start interacting with the organization, those interactions create another layer of intelligence.

Useful signals can include:

- Pages viewed
- Forms submitted
- Resources downloaded
- Emails opened or clicked
- Event interactions
- Repeat visits
- Product interest
- Content categories consumed
- Lead source
- Lifecycle stage
- CRM activity
- Enrichment data
- Sales activity

The important thing is not collecting every possible data point.

It's identifying the signals that actually help answer useful questions:

- Who is this person or organization?
- How relevant are they?
- What are they interested in?
- Where are they in the journey?
- What should happen next?

## Build an audience hierarchy

Not everyone you communicate with is necessarily a direct buyer.

I find it useful to separate audiences into categories.

### Primary audiences

The people or organizations most directly connected to the commercial or organizational objective.

These usually receive the greatest focus around:

- Qualification
- Conversion
- Sales handoff
- Attribution
- Lifecycle management

### Secondary audiences

People who may influence the primary audience, create demand, shape reputation, educate the market, or affect adoption.

They may never become the direct customer, but they can still have substantial strategic value.

### Tertiary audiences

Broader audiences that may matter for:

- Awareness
- Education
- Community
- Reputation
- Long-term influence

but are less directly attributable to the main objective.

This distinction matters because different audiences require different:

- Content
- Calls to action
- Channels
- Measurement
- Nurture
- Expectations around attribution

A secondary audience should not automatically be treated like a primary buyer simply because they generate traffic.

## ICPs should be dynamic

I prefer to think of an ICP as a combination of attributes rather than one static persona.

A simplified way to think about the audience model:

| Layer | Question |
|---|---|
| **Identity** | Who are they? |
| **Fit** | What characteristics matter? |
| **Declared intent** | What have they told us? |
| **Enrichment** | What have we learned about them? |
| **Behavior** | What are they doing? |
| **Decision** | How should we treat them? |

The first five layers create the context. The final layer determines the action.
This allows audiences to become increasingly specific without having to create hundreds of static persona documents.

For example, the system might distinguish between organizations based on:

- Type
- Size
- Geography
- Capability
- Technology
- Need
- Intent
- Engagement

Those combinations can then determine:

- Which content someone receives
- Which audience list they enter
- Which workflow runs
- Whether they are nurtured
- Whether Sales is notified
- How they are prioritized
- How performance is measured

As more data becomes available, the model should evolve.

New customer conversations can create new CRM fields.

New search behavior can reveal new needs.

New lead patterns can change qualification criteria.

New sales feedback can improve routing.

The system should learn.

## Build content from evidence, not guesses

Once the audience model exists, content priorities should come from multiple inputs rather than one source.

I generally think about:

### Content prioritization inputs

| Input | What it tells us |
|---|---|
| **Customer needs** | What problems actually need solving |
| **Sales & SME expertise** | What we know from real conversations and specialist knowledge |
| **Search demand** | What people are actively looking for |
| **Business priorities** | What the organization needs to grow or support |
| **Available resources** | What we can realistically execute well |

The strongest opportunities usually sit at the intersection of several of these signals.

This allows content to serve several purposes at once:

- Answer real questions
- Improve discoverability
- Build authority
- Support strategic priorities
- Educate potential customers
- Support Sales
- Create conversion opportunities

### Broad demand and niche demand

Sometimes broader educational content can help establish visibility and authority before more commercially specific topics become realistic opportunities.

A simplified approach:

Broad useful content  
↓  
Organic visibility  
↓  
Authority  
↓  
More audience data  
↓  
More specific opportunities  
↓  
Niche high-intent content  
↓  
Qualified demand

The objective isn't traffic for the sake of traffic.

The broad content should strengthen the wider system and create opportunities to reach more specific audiences over time.

## Subject-matter expertise and validation

In technical, scientific, regulated, or otherwise specialist industries, Marketing should not pretend to be the expert.

A better process is:

Audience / search opportunity  
↓  
Marketing research  
↓  
Subject-matter expert input  
↓  
Content development  
↓  
Technical validation  
↓  
Publication  
↓  
Performance feedback

This can improve:

- Accuracy
- Credibility
- Depth
- Originality
- Search usefulness
- AI citability
- Customer trust

It also creates a way to turn knowledge that already exists inside an organization into useful public resources.

The challenge is creating a validation process that protects accuracy without making publishing impossibly slow.

---

## The feedback loop

The most useful part of this approach is that audience research does not end once content or campaigns launch.

It becomes a loop:

Customer conversations  
↓  
Sales & internal knowledge  
↓  
Structured audience data  
↓  
Search & market research  
↓  
Content and distribution  
↓  
Traffic and engagement  
↓  
Leads and customer behavior  
↓  
CRM and sales outcomes  
↓  
New audience intelligence  
↺

That means demand generation itself becomes a form of market research.

Every new interaction can potentially improve the next iteration of the system.

## A practical working template

Before building a campaign, content plan, or audience strategy, I try to get reasonably clear answers to questions like these:

| Question                                                                                           | Notes / Inputs |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
| Who are we trying to reach?                                                                        |                |
| Are they buyers, influencers, users, or something else?                                            |                |
| What characteristics make them relevant?                                                           |                |
| What would make one member of this audience more valuable or relevant than another?                |                |
| What problems come up repeatedly?                                                                  |                |
| What language do customers use to describe those problems?                                         |                |
| What situations cause them to start looking for help?                                              |                |
| What are they searching for?                                                                       |                |
| What questions appear repeatedly in search?                                                        |                |
| What is Sales hearing that Marketing may not be seeing?                                            |                |
| What knowledge exists with subject-matter experts that hasn't been turned into useful content yet? |                |
| What does our CRM data tell us?                                                                    |                |
| What does behavioral data tell us?                                                                 |                |
| What data are we missing that would make segmentation or qualification better?                     |                |
| What is the organization trying to prioritize?                                                     |                |
| Where do audience demand and organizational priorities overlap?                                    |                |
| What useful information, tool, resource, or experience could we create?                            |                |
| How could that asset be reused across channels?                                                    |                |
| What should happen when someone engages with it?                                                   |                |
| How will we know if it worked?                                                                     |                |

You don't need perfect answers to all of these before doing anything.

The point is to make the assumptions visible, use the information you already have, and keep improving the picture as more data comes in.

## A few things I've learned

### Start with real people

Personas and ICPs are only useful if they reflect actual customers, conversations, behavior, and data. A workshop can help organize what you know, but it shouldn't be the source of truth.

### A lot of customer knowledge is already inside the organization

Sales, technical teams, customer-facing teams, and subject-matter experts usually know things that never make it into the CRM, the content plan, or the reporting.

Finding ways to capture and structure that knowledge has been one of the most useful parts of this process.

### Search tells you more than what to write about

Keyword research is obviously useful for SEO, but it's also a window into how people describe their problems, what they don't understand, and what they're actively trying to figure out.

### Traffic isn't all the same

Some audiences buy. Some influence the buyer. Some help build awareness or credibility.

They can all matter, but they shouldn't all be measured or treated the same way.

### Volume isn't the strategy

The biggest keyword or broadest audience isn't automatically the best opportunity.

Search demand has to be weighed against relevance, business priorities, expertise, resources, and what you can actually do something useful with.

### Keep changing the model

The audience you start with won't be exactly the audience you understand a year later.

New leads, customer conversations, search behavior, sales feedback, and CRM data should keep changing how you segment, prioritize, and communicate.
